# Research notes: FITNESS-STUDIO sites (pilates, yoga, barre, reformer, boutique classes)

Scope: public home, class and single-studio pages of Club Pilates, [solidcore], Barry's
and Equinox; small independent pilates studio sites as gathered by two inspiration galleries
(Squarespace / Wix / Framer / Webflow builds); recommendations from studio-software vendors
(Mindbody, bsport); and how small studios in Korea say they take enquiries. Researched
November 2026. Live pages were read through a text fetcher, so only structure, order and
labels were seen, not exact visuals. Where a note says "seen", a fetched page showed it.

## 1. What the first screen does
- The single strongest pattern: ONE first-visit action in the header and the hero. Club
  Pilates uses "Try a Free Intro Class" / "Book a Free Intro Class"; Barry's uses "Book Your
  First Class" in the header AND the hero; [solidcore] uses "Book now" / "Book class" with a
  small "New to ...?" link underneath (src: [CP-HOME], [BARRYS], [SOLID]).
- Hero = a short headline (3-6 words), one sentence on the format (e.g. 50-minute full-body
  class), a primary button, and a quieter secondary link ("Learn more about the workout",
  "New to us?") (src: [SOLID], [BARRYS]).
- Imagery is real people in class on the equipment: a group on reformers [SOLID], a looping
  class video with a pause control [BARRYS]. Small studios mostly use a full-width photo,
  some a split photo layout or video (src: [COLORLIB]). Vendors stress real photos of the
  space and the community over stock (src: [MBO]).
- Booking CTA should be visible without scrolling and stay visible (pinned header button)
  (src: [BSPORT], [COLORLIB] "fixed-header Book a Class button").
- Location belongs in the first screen and the page title for local search (src: [MBO]).
- Chains add a promo strip above the header (HSA/FSA, first-month discount) (src: [CP-HOME]).
  For a single small studio this is optional; one current offer at most (inferred).

## 2. Home page order (seen across leaders)
- Club Pilates: promo strip, header, hero + offer, trust numbers, "which visitor are you"
  cards (new / member / returning), 3-step "how it works" with mini FAQs, class carousel
  (intro class first), studio finder (src: [CP-HOME]).
- [solidcore]: hero, three "difference" points with a button, promos, monthly focus, app,
  closing "start today" band, footer (src: [SOLID]).
- Barry's: hero video, two class-format cards with a category tag, short feature blocks,
  instructor block with first-timer advice, amenities cards, testimonials, newsletter form
  with a "first time?" question and consent box, footer (src: [BARRYS]).
- Equinox: hero offer, then alternating image/text modules (locations, membership,
  classes as a tile grid, extra services as cards) (src: [EQX]).
- Shared skeleton (inferred): hero with first-visit CTA -> what makes the method / studio
  different (3 points) -> class types as cards -> how a first visit works -> people
  (instructors) -> proof (reviews) -> location / contact -> closing CTA band -> footer.

## 3. Classes
- Classes are named cards: photo, name, 1-2 sentence description, sometimes a category tag
  (cardio / strength) (src: [CP-CLASSES], [BARRYS], [EQX]).
- The beginner / intro class is placed first and labelled as the starting point
  (src: [CP-HOME], [CP-CLASSES]).
- Levels explained in a separate "Which level is right for you?" section, each level a
  short block saying who it suits (src: [CP-CLASSES]).
- Private sessions get their own block with benefits and "call or contact the studio"
  rather than a booking button (src: [CP-CLASSES]).
- A short practical FAQ (what to wear, arrive early, injuries) sits on the classes page
  (src: [CP-CLASSES]).
- Weakness seen: chain class cards do not show duration, level or price on the card
  (src: [CP-CLASSES], [EQX]). bsport recommends specific type and level descriptions
  (src: [BSPORT]). Showing duration, level, capacity and price on every card is better for
  a small site (inferred).

## 4. Pricing
- Chains hide normal prices behind a membership page; the home page shows only the offer
  (src: [CP-HOME], [EQX], [BARRYS]).
- Single-studio pages show 3-4 plan cards with a label, an optional badge ("Most popular",
  "Best value"), title and bullet features (src: [CP-STUDIO]).
- Vendors: show prices and tiers openly, highlight savings on bigger packs, put the intro
  offer on the pricing page, state cancellation and contract terms and fees up front
  (src: [BSPORT], [MBO]). A vendor claim that 58% leave without finding pricing has an
  unclear source (src: [VENDOR-BLOGS]); treat as indicative only.
- Korean small studios list a trial lesson price (e.g. from 50,000-55,000 won), note that
  1:1 prices depend on the number of sessions and are set after a consultation, and state
  a cancellation window (e.g. 18 hours before) (src: [KR-LOCAL]).

## 5. Instructors
- Chains treat instructors as one collective block ("best-in-class instructors") with
  first-timer reassurance (src: [BARRYS]); studio pages list instructors per studio
  (src: [CP-STUDIO], content loaded dynamically, fields not seen).
- Vendors: write personal, descriptive bios so visitors find someone they relate to
  (src: [BSPORT]); certifications are a trust signal (src: [COLORLIB] BASI host studio,
  certified instructors as benefit icon).
- For a small studio the instructors ARE the brand: portrait, name, role, certifications,
  what their class feels like, which classes they teach (inferred).

## 6. Booking, contact and location
- Book buttons go straight to a schedule or studio picker, never through a contact form
  (src: [BSPORT], [BARRYS] all booking -> studio selection).
- 20% of surveyed members said an unclear booking process would stop them trying a studio
  (src: [BSPORT], vendor survey of 550).
- Studio page: address under the name, rating, email link, phone link, opening hours,
  "Get directions" opening an external map (no embedded map), first-visit checklist,
  reviews with name + "member since" (src: [CP-STUDIO]).
- Vendors: show address and parking, embed a map, answer first-visit questions on the page;
  offer more than one way to connect: phone, chat, form with fast response, walk-in
  (src: [BSPORT], [MBO]).
- Contact forms on chain home pages: none, or only a newsletter-style form asking name,
  email, studio, first time yes/no, consent (src: [BARRYS], [EQX]).
- Small studios commonly book through a third-party scheduler (Momence, Mindbody) linked or
  embedded (src: [COLORLIB], [SBR]); vendors warn against redirects that break the flow and
  advise styling embedded widgets to match (src: [VENDOR-BLOGS]).
- Korea: small studios take enquiries via KakaoTalk channel and Naver TalkTalk, run
  consultations and classes "by reservation only", and are found through Naver Place / Naver
  booking (src: [KR-LOCAL]). So a Korean studio site should show call, KakaoTalk and Naver
  Map / Kakao Map links next to the form (inferred from [KR-LOCAL]).

## 7. First-visit reassurance
- Club Pilates: three steps (book online, arrive early, take a guided class), each with a
  mini FAQ (fitness level, attire, length) (src: [CP-HOME]); a first-visit checklist on the
  studio page (src: [CP-STUDIO]).
- Barry's instructor block includes first-timer guidance; class cards give a "pro tip"
  alternative for those who cannot run (src: [BARRYS]).
- Vendors: speak to one audience and name their worries (src: [BSPORT]); a "Get started"
  page combining FAQ, testimonials, offers and pricing (src: [MBO]).

## 8. Style
- Pilates sites mostly lean muted: warm earth tones, nature palettes, calm tones; editorial
  or refined serif type; overlapping photo blocks; marquee banners (src: [COLORLIB]).
- Deliberate exceptions stand out: lime green accent, blue and pink, purple, peach with
  sparkle graphics (src: [COLORLIB]); bold oversized type with strong imagery (src:
  [COLORLIB] Carve). bsport: calm and earthy fits pilates, bold and energetic fits spin
  (src: [BSPORT]).
- Builders: 26 of 36 gallery sites on Squarespace, 4 Wix, 4 Framer, 2 WordPress (src: [SBR]);
  i.e. most small studio sites are template-based single-scroll pages with a few subpages.
- High-intensity brands use dark rooms, red or neon light and video (src: [BARRYS]
  "The Red Room") (inferred for the look).

## 9. Numbers: conventions vs specs
- None of the numbers below are official specs. Header height 64-80px, hero 70-90vh,
  card radius 8-16px, 3-up card grids at desktop, 44px touch targets (WCAG 2.5.5 AAA /
  platform guidance), pinned book button: these are conventions (inferred from common
  practice). Survey and conversion figures come from vendors (bsport, Mindbody) and are
  marked as such above.
