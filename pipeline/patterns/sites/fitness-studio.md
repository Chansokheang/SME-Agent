# Site guide: FITNESS-STUDIO (pilates, yoga, barre, reformer and other class-based studios)

Studied: Club Pilates, [solidcore], Barry's, Equinox, Mindbody, bsport

People come to a studio site to answer five questions, usually on a phone, often from a map
or social link: What kind of class is this, and is it for someone like me? Where is it?
What does it cost? Who teaches? How do I start? The leading studios (Club Pilates,
[solidcore], Barry's, Equinox) put ONE first-visit action first, in the header and the hero
("book your first class", "try an intro class", or for a small studio "ask about a trial
lesson"). The class types come straight after, then a calm explanation of the first visit,
the people who teach, proof from members, and finally the address and a way to make
contact. The best small independent studios (mostly Squarespace, Wix and Framer sites)
follow the same order on one long home page with a few short subpages. They set themselves
apart through photography and type, not through new structure. Evidence:
`out/research/fitness-studio-research.md` (researched Nov 2026).

## What the first screen shows
- **Header (64-80px, pinned on scroll)**: logo or wordmark left; 3-5 text links (Classes,
  Instructors, Pricing if it is a separate page, Contact); on the right ONE filled button
  for the first-visit action ("Book a trial class", "Ask about a trial"). If the studio
  takes calls, a phone icon-link sits beside it on phones. No login link unless members
  actually log in here.
- **Hero (about 70-90vh on desktop, shorter on phones so the next section peeks in)**:
  - A real photo or a short muted looping video of a class in the studio's own room, people
    on the equipment, natural light. A pause control if it is video.
  - A headline of 3-7 words about the feeling or result, not the brand name.
  - One line on what the studio is and where: "Small-group reformer and mat pilates in
    [neighbourhood]." The neighbourhood name belongs in this first screen.
  - Primary button (the first-visit action) and a quieter secondary link ("See classes",
    "New to pilates?").
  - Optional: a single line of proof under the buttons (rating, years open, maximum class
    size such as "Up to 6 people per class").
- **At most one offer** (a trial price or first-week offer) as a small band or a line in the
  hero. Chains stack several promo strips; a small studio should not.

## The main flow, step by step
1. **Arrive** (from map, search, Instagram, KakaoTalk or a friend's link) on the home page or
   straight on Classes.
2. **Understand the classes**: class-type cards (group mat, reformer, private, etc.), each
   with level, length, group size and starting price, beginner option first.
3. **Get reassured**: "Your first visit" in 3 steps (contact or book -> arrive 10 minutes
   early, what to wear -> a guided first class), plus 3-5 short FAQs (no experience? injuries?
   what to bring?).
4. **Meet the teachers**: portraits and short bios; trust grows here.
5. **Check practicals**: price list, address, opening hours, nearest station, parking.
6. **Act**: book a trial through the booking tool, OR call / message / send the form. Every
   "book" or "try" button goes straight to the schedule or contact form, never through an
   extra page. Aim for at most 3 taps from the home page to a sent request.
7. **Done**: a clear confirmation page or message saying what happens next and when the
   studio will reply ("We reply within one working day, usually by phone or KakaoTalk").

## Navigation and structure
- **Typical pages**: Home, Classes (with prices, or a separate Pricing page), Instructors
  (or Team), Contact / Visit, Privacy. Add Schedule if there is a live timetable, FAQ or
  "New here" if the FAQ is long. Five to seven pages total; small studios rarely need more.
- **Header links**: the pages above in that order, the action button last. Schedule, pricing
  and classes must be visible names, never hidden under "Information" or "About".
- **Mobile**: logo left, the action button (compact) and a menu button right. The menu opens
  as a full-height sheet with large links (at least 48px rows), then call, message and
  address at the bottom. Optionally a slim bottom bar with "Call" and "Book / Message" on
  phones, kept out of the way of the footer.
- **Footer** (dense but calm, 3-4 columns on desktop, stacked on phones): address with a map
  link, phone, email, messaging channel links, opening hours, social icons, the page links,
  business registration details where local law expects them (Korea: business name,
  representative, registration number), Privacy, copyright.
- **Local channels**: show the channels people in the market really use. In Korea that
  means a phone link, a KakaoTalk channel link, Naver Map and Kakao Map links, and Naver
  booking if the studio uses it. Elsewhere it means Google Maps and WhatsApp or Instagram DM.

## The key components
- **Class-type card** (grid of 3 at desktop, 1 column with horizontal photos on phones):
  photo of that class (16:10 or 4:5), name, one-line who-it-is-for, then a quiet facts row:
  level ("All levels", "Beginner friendly"), length ("50 min"), group size ("Up to 6"),
  price "from" per class. One link: "Details" or "Ask about this class". Mark the starting
  class with a small label ("Start here"). The chains omit length and price from the cards;
  include them.
- **Class detail block** (on the Classes page, one section per type, alternating
  photo/text): what happens in a class, who it suits and who should start elsewhere,
  equipment, the facts row, and a price table for that type.
- **Price table**: grouped by class type; rows of "1 class / 10 classes / monthly", price
  right-aligned, per-class price as a muted second line, one row highlighted as the usual
  choice. Below: trial price, validity of passes, cancellation window ("Cancel or move a
  class up to 24 hours before"), and what is included (mat, towels). Prices with the
  currency and "incl. VAT" where relevant. If 1:1 prices depend on a consultation, say so
  plainly with a "from" price instead of hiding them.
- **Level guide**: 3-4 short blocks or a simple table: level name, who it suits, which
  classes. Keeps beginners from guessing.
- **First-visit steps**: 3 numbered columns (stacked on phones), each a short title and 1-2
  sentences, followed by an accordion of 3-6 FAQs.
- **Instructor card**: portrait (4:5, same crop and light for every teacher), name, role,
  certifications in one muted line, a 2-4 sentence bio in the first person or a warm third
  person, the classes they teach as small tags. On a dedicated page, alternate
  portrait-left / portrait-right or use a 3-up grid with the bio below.
- **Testimonials**: 3 short quotes with first name and how long they have been a member,
  or a rating summary linking to the map listing. Real names and real photos only.
- **Schedule (when the studio has one)**: day tabs or a 7-day strip on top; for each day a
  list of rows: time, class name, level, instructor, spots left, and a "Book" or "Waitlist"
  button. Embedded booking tools (Momence, Mindbody, bsport, Naver booking) are common;
  style them to match and keep them on the site rather than redirecting where possible.
- **Visit / contact block**: map image or embedded map (lazy-loaded), address with a copy
  button, nearest station and exit, walking minutes, parking, opening hours as a two-column
  list, then buttons: Call, Message (KakaoTalk / WhatsApp), Open in maps.
- **Contact form**: name, phone or email (the preferred contact method), the class they are
  interested in (select: group mat / reformer / private / not sure), preferred times (optional
  chips: mornings, lunchtime, evenings, weekends), message, a required privacy consent box
  linking to the privacy page, and a button saying what happens ("Send enquiry"). Phone and
  message options appear BESIDE the form, not only under it.
- **Closing CTA band**: one sentence and the primary button, above the footer on every page.

## States and messages
- **Form sending**: the button shows a spinner and "Sending...", disabled to stop double
  sends; the typed text stays.
- **Form errors**: inline under each field in plain words ("Please add a phone number or
  email so we can reply"), the first error focused, never cleared input.
- **Form success**: replace the form with a confirmation: thanks, what happens next, how soon
  the reply comes, and the phone number for anything urgent. Do not use a toast that
  disappears.
- **Send failure**: keep the message, say it did not send, offer Retry and the phone or
  messaging link.
- **Class full / no spots** (if a schedule is shown): "Full" with "Join waitlist" instead of
  a disabled button; "2 spots left" in a muted warning tone.
- **Studio closed / holidays**: a dated notice band ("Closed 1-3 January") at the top of the
  home and contact pages, removed after the date.
- **Outdated info**: a "Prices updated [month year]" line under the price table; an old
  timetable looks like a closed business.
- **Empty states**: if a teacher has no photo yet, use a neutral placeholder with initials,
  never a stock face.

## What the leaders get right
- One first-visit action, repeated in the header, hero, after the class section and in the
  closing band, worded the same each time.
- The beginner path is explicit: an intro or trial class first, a level guide, a
  "your first visit" explainer that answers what to wear and when to arrive.
- Real photos of the actual room, equipment and people.
- Address, phone and hours on the studio page; directions open the visitor's map app.
- Reviews tied to real members (name, member since).

## Common mistakes to avoid
- Hiding prices entirely, or showing only "contact us for pricing" for group classes.
- "Book" buttons that lead to a generic contact form, or a redirect to a different-looking
  booking site with no way back.
- Class cards with only a name and a stock photo; no level, length or group size.
- Stock photos of models in a gym that is not this studio.
- An enormous auto-playing video with sound, or hero text unreadable over a busy photo.
- A schedule screenshot image (unreadable on phones, always out of date).
- Contact page with a form only: no phone, no map link, no hours, no station.
- Several pop-ups (newsletter, offer, chat) on the first visit.
- Instructor bios that are a list of certificates with no personality, or teachers with
  mismatched photo crops.

## Style: what this kind usually looks like, and where there is room to be different
- **The usual pilates and yoga look**: calm and light. Warm off-white or sand backgrounds,
  earthy or muted accent (clay, sage, stone, soft blush), lots of space, an editorial serif
  or refined sans for headings with a clean sans for text, large real photos with soft
  natural light, gentle rounded corners (8-16px) or none, thin dividers, almost no shadows.
  Section padding is generous (80-128px desktop, 56-72px phones). Text 16-18px.
- **The high-intensity look** (spin, HIIT, strength): dark backgrounds, one loud accent
  (red, neon), heavy condensed type, video, energetic motion.
- **Room to be different**: a single bold accent colour on an otherwise calm site (lime,
  cobalt, tomato) stands out among muted competitors; oversized type with strong
  photography; overlapping or offset photo blocks for an editorial feel; a slow marquee of
  class names; illustration or motif drawn from the neighbourhood. Keep the structure
  familiar and let the style carry the personality.
- **Motion**: slow, quiet fades and small rises (300-500ms) on scroll, gentle hover lift on
  cards, no parallax that fights reading, everything respectful of reduced motion.
- **Accessibility**: text over photos needs a scrim to reach 4.5:1; buttons at least 44px
  tall; video has pause; the map always has a text address next to it; form fields have
  visible labels (not placeholder-only); phone numbers are real `tel:` links.
